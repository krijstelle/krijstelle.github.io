Find'em - User recruitment

A mini service design project to improve participant recruitment for research.

01.-

The start

Not long after I started working in RCA (realcommercial.com.au), I soon realised conducting research can be quite challenging with commercial consumers. In REA, normally the process was to reach out to a recruitment agency to find participants that fit our research criteria. Sometimes the session was very insightful, sometimes not so much because we didn’t get the right profile. Sometimes we find the participants recruited didn’t reflect real users as many participants would say they’re just casually looking.  Unlike residential properties, not everybody has experience in commercial properties. With less people in the pool, it’s harder to find the right people to recruit. Not getting quality research insights from interview sessions was not only frustrating but also very costly to the business.

02.-

Hack day: Firebase notification

I started brainstorming with the team about how we can improve the process. In 2017, during one of the hack days, I worked on a project called Find’em with my dev team. The idea was to target relevant users using Firebase, then using the app to send out an in-app invitation (notification) to participate for further research. In firebase, each user has a unique user ID. We were able to analyse user behaviour, based on their interaction with the app. It not only gave us a great idea of usage of the features, also allow us to filter down a number of user actions before triggering the notification.

For example, if we were to re-design our property alerts, we can make sure we target people who are logged in, frequently use the app, with a few properties saved- to make sure they have experience with the current property alerts feature. We then send a notification out to invite them to participate research. 

After the hack day we continue to work on this to tie up the loose ends. For example, getting legal advice and sign-off, as well as working on the survey. However at this point, we realised the amount of work needed after getting the initial survey was huge. To be able to make sure the participant fits our screening criteria, we need to know more than just their in-app interaction. We decided to park the project and re-think the value of investing our time on recruitment.  

03.-

Consumer satisfaction survey

The idea of recruiting better quality participants didn’t go away, even after a few months. During these few months, I tried tightening recruitment brief, re-write our normal screening criteria. 

The lightbulb moment 

Consumer satisfaction survey is a survey the team uses annually to measure the performance of the app and understand our user from a quantitative level.  We included the last question to opt in for further research in 2019. This way we can be sure participants are definitely our app users. To our surprise, many users opted in. Thanks for the market research team, we were able to filter the right samples to reach out based on their survey response. 

The process involved writing a screening script and contacting a list of participants. The screening script would confirm their survey response and their availability. Once the interview is set up, we send through a confirmation email with a zoom link. Few days before the research, I would also email the link as a reminder with prototype links, and consent form. 

04.-

The outcome

High quality participants  

We were pleasantly surprised by the positive outcome we have seen in the latest round of research. These participants were more than happy to provide feedback with nothing in return. Having said that, we did offer incentives after each session. The point here is to have people who aren’t there just for the incentive. All participants were very engaged and provide lots valuable insights. 

Building an internal pool of user (cost saving) 

As using a recruitment agency is very costly, this process helps the business to save thousands of dollars from paying the recruitment agency. We also got to curate a pool of participants we can reach out to for different research purposes.

The participants segments were in line with our analytic data 

Warehouse, Retail and Land development are the top 3 searched categories in the app (accroding to Mixpanel). Previously with the recruitment agency we had trouble recruiting. We tend to end up interviewing office seekers, which doesn’t actually reflect our primary user based. This time we got to speak to more people looking for land development, which has been a change for us.
