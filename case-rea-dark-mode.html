CASE STUDY · REA GROUP

Dark Mode

A fun collaborative project with the developers to explore dark mode when it was first introduced at WWDC 2019.

Role
Senior Product Designer
Company
REA Group
Focus
Design systems, accessibility, motion

01. The beginning

Should we implement dark mode?

In 2019, Apple released iOS 13, which brought dark mode to apps that chose to adopt it. As an app team, we began to ask ourselves: should we implement dark mode?

I wanted to understand the value of dark mode before jumping straight into the design, so I picked up some research in between projects. Understanding the value helped me see the benefit of dark mode as a feature, and helped the team prioritise it in our backlog.

02. The research

Peak search hours are between 8pm and 10pm

Many movie apps already have dark mode. As most people watch movies at night, dark screens are easier on the eyes. From Mixpanel, we knew our app gets the most traffic at night. I saw an opportunity for dark mode, because people like to do a bit of searching on the app before going to bed.

Supporting dark mode also brings another user benefit: saving battery. Research shows that at full screen brightness, dark mode uses 43% less power than light mode. Black on OLED screens uses less power because the LEDs that make up each pixel are switched off, whereas displaying white means the LEDs need to shine and consume power.

03. Design process

Cross-team collaboration

Working on dark mode gave me the opportunity to share knowledge and work with other app designers across the business. It was so much fun! Different teams had different views on the size of this project, so we started by defining the design principles for this piece of work. Even though each app under REA has its own identity, we wanted the new theme to be consistent at a group level. Part of the design principles (and the obvious one) was to make sure we share the same colour palette.

Colour palette: first pass

What colour should we use for dark mode? I gave it a go by picking the primary dark colour (Shadowstone) from our current component library. I thought reusing the colour would make our dark mode feel more on brand. I tried both dark blue and black. From the feedback I received, dark blue was the preferred option because people felt it was easier on the eyes.

However, when we placed the dark blue on a scale alongside other apps with dark mode, our initial palette turned out to be on the lightest side. That wasn’t ideal, because dark mode shouldn’t be too light.

The working group got together and revisited the colour palette. The goal was to find the middle point between dark blue and black. We iterated on a few options, and in the end landed on a palette we were happy to use as a reference.

04. Development handover

System background vs content colours

I then passed the palette to the developer in my team. After giving it a go, we noticed it didn’t look quite right. If we just followed the palette, some of the design principles in light mode couldn’t be translated to dark mode.

From conversations with the developers in the team, I learnt that in code you can set your default background colours. We needed to separate our approach for background colours and content. This allowed us to translate the elevated views in light mode into dark mode. I came up with a simple graph to communicate this with the team, and it was shared across the business for other designers to use with their developers. The rule of thumb is to set up primary, secondary and tertiary background colours, then everything else follows the colour palette.

05. The impact

Users are using dark mode in our app

We saw close to 30% uptake just 2 weeks after the release in October 2019. For users with dark mode enabled, usage has increased steadily. While usage without dark mode is still higher, dark mode usage keeps rising. The goal of this project wasn’t to convert users to dark mode, but to improve the experience of the app. Seeing the share of users adopting dark mode gave us the confidence to iterate on the UI further. With popular apps (such as Instagram) slowly phasing in dark mode, we’ll keep tracking any uptake on the back of this.

Since launching the first iteration, I’ve kept making updates, such as making sure all the minor details pass accessibility requirements.

06. Key learnings

Socialise new ideas early

I’m very happy with the outcome and the process of this project. I’m glad I didn’t treat it as ‘another UI card’. I started by understanding why dark mode is a thing and how it could benefit our app users. This helped me bring the team along on the journey and see the value of the change.

Accessibility check

One thing I learnt while designing for dark mode is to take accessibility checks more seriously. They’re just as important as in light mode, and even more so in dark mode, because we need to make sure the colour contrast is readable. Some components needed to be assessed case by case. Sometimes we needed to soften the colours in dark mode so everything on the page looked cohesive.

Dark mode is now part of the design and QA process

Moving forward, dark mode is part of the design process. Every new feature we design gets tested in dark mode. Just like testing on different device sizes, we want to make sure the implementation works well in dark mode, and adjust the design to accommodate it.
