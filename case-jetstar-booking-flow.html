CASE STUDY · JETSTAR

Booking flow redesign

Creating a design vision Jetstar’s booking teams could rally around

Role
Senior product designer
Team
PM, Engineering
Company
Jetstar
Timeline
18 months

01. The challenge

The roadmap that didn’t exist

Sometimes projects don’t come in from the roadmap.

Instead, there were just project requests from the commercial team focusing on sales targets. Customer experience was an afterthought. Together, they piled more onto an already crowded flow, and nobody owned the overall customer experience.

I noticed the problem wasn’t a lack of ideas. It was a lack of a shared picture of what customer experience we would like to build towards. This project was a puzzle I assembled one piece at a time, starting from building trust with my team, then slowly building the full picture of our vision together.

02. Research & Framing

From customer insights to testable bets

Before jumping into solutions, I worked with our researcher to run a 4 parts customer research to understand where booking broke down.

Three opportunities stood out:

  • Choice without repetition:
  • Faster checkout with price transparency:
  • Bundle clarity with contextual upsells:

I introduced the opportunity solution tree to the team and used these three insights as its branches. With the PM, I mapped every open request and idea under them, tying each one to a customer need and a measurable business outcome.

Then I worked with my PM to prioritise the best way to sequence the experiments, based on customer impact, commercial value, and effort.

This was the turning point. Commercial saw their goals reflected in the tree. Engineering could see scope early. The team moved from debating opinions to agreeing on experiments.

03. Prototyping

Putting the pieces together

Each idea worked on its own, but we didn’t know if they worked together. When Figma Make arrived, I used it to combine every solution from our tree into one end-to-end prototype in days instead of weeks. Testing the full flow showed us what customers valued most, and that’s where our principles came from.

The principles that emerged

Across every round of testing, three principles kept proving true. They became the guardrails for future booking work:

Progressive disclosure

Show customers what they need now. Everything else can wait.

Simplified decisions

Reduce the number of choices. The basic fare is in every booking, so customers shouldn’t have to pick it. Three bundles beat four.

Show it when it matters

Nobody reads upsell banners that aren’t relevant. If we want people to join the membership, show them their instant savings at the moment they apply.

A shorter and smoother flow

Currently customers jump through multiple hoops to complete their booking. By following the principles, we were able to reduce the number of pages from 7 to 4.

04. Testing

Solutions, tested one by one

We A/B tested each idea on its own to measure the impact and minimise commercial risks:

  1. Test 01

    Bundle cards uplift

    Making bundle inclusions easier to digest.

  2. Test 02

    Exclusive pricing

    Show the value of getting a bundle.

  3. Test 03

    One bundle select

    94% of the customers choose the same bundle, they shouldn’t be doing the same action twice.

  4. Test 04

    Bags page forward

    Streamline the decision-making process.

  5. Test 05

    Contextual upsells

    Push for upgrade or membership only when it’s relevant.

05. The outcome

A shared vision, and a team that works differently

A design vision for booking

The team got excited by the future booking experience, adopted by the wider digital team.

Stronger cross-functional collaboration

A visual direction replaced abstract debates. Commercial, Product and Engineering could react to the same thing and align faster.

From delivery-focused to product-focused

The team shifted from working through requests to shaping problems, testing hypotheses, and measuring outcomes. Long-term strategy starts to align and plan for a way forward.

What I’ve learned about leading design

  1. Through building trust with key project partners early on, we can achieve amazing things together.
  2. Use an opportunity solution tree to keep everyone focused on the outcome, not on building more stuff.
  3. Let customer feedback decide, not opinions. Test early, then iterate.
  4. A vision prototype works wonders when it comes to cross-functional collaboration.
